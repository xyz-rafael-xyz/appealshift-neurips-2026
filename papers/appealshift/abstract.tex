\title{Matched Source Controls Expose a Capability Boundary in Appeal Review}

\begin{abstract}
Plausible evidence can still be inadmissible under a service policy, and apparent accuracy depends on the disposition convention used to score it. We pair 48 synthetic humanitarian appeals from unlisted sources with 24 controls from accepted sources. Under the printed policy, four local models return the exact invalid-source disposition in 0 of 384 independent reviews. An alternative information-request target changes the model ranking. Mistral's independent balanced exact score rises from 38.5 to 85.4 percent, and Phi's rises from 25.0 to 62.5 percent. Qwen and Gemma remain constant acceptors. GPT-5.6 Sol separates all 96 unlisted records from all 48 accepted controls under both review contexts. It returns the printed disposition with 100 percent balanced exact accuracy, while the alternative convention scores the same outputs at 50 percent. Every frontier answer is strictly parsed and fully grounded. Earlier-rationale exposure raises local Mistral's false eligibility by 35.42 points, with a 12-base-request interval of [29.17, 41.67]. The fictional cases support no operational aid decision. For peace-oriented review, we define a three-step Matched Admissibility Audit. It pairs accepted with unlisted sources. The audit scores the exact disposition under a declared convention before repeating across prompt order and model revision.
\end{abstract}
